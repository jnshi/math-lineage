\paragraph{What this document reports.}
Each finding is a load-bearing part of the preprint (a technique,
construction, estimate, proof route or result) that an identifiable earlier
paper already contains, where the preprint does not cite that paper for it at
the place it is used. The one exception is overstated novelty, described
below. Textbook tools and ``should cite the original rather than a later
source'' quibbles are not reported. Findings are listed by centrality, then by
how close the relationship is.

\paragraph{Relationships.}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.5em,font=\normalfont\itshape]
\item[same technique.] The earlier technique, construction or theory is
reused essentially intact, possibly in a new setting or with new ingredients
around it.
\item[adapted.] The earlier technique's structure is followed, with a change
at its core: a different object, or a key step replaced.
\item[fragment.] One identifiable piece of the earlier technique is reused.
\item[same route.] An earlier paper proposed the proof strategy, even
heuristically, and the preprint carries it out.
\item[same result.] An earlier paper proves essentially the same statement.
\item[weaker result.] An earlier paper proves a weaker, special-case or
partial-range version of a statement the preprint proves.
\item[subsuming result.] A published result would replace or substantially
shorten a large part of the argument.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Centrality.}
Each finding also gets a best-effort judgment of how much of the preprint
depends on it: \emph{main result} when the earlier work covers the main result
itself, or most of it; \emph{central} when the proof of the main theorem runs
through it; \emph{supporting} when it is an input to a central step;
\emph{peripheral} otherwise. An earlier weaker result takes the centrality of
the statement it precedes, at most central. Each finding states the chain of
results it feeds, so the judgment can be checked.

\paragraph{Citations.}
The ``Credited?'' column of the summary names the earlier work and says what
the preprint cites for the content in question: \emph{not cited} (the work is
not in the bibliography), \emph{cited for other content}, \emph{credited
elsewhere} (cited for this content, but not where it is used), or
\emph{credited in part}. A citation counts only when it is \emph{for that
specific content}; citing the same paper nearby for a different result does
not count. Content the preprint credits where it is used, directly or through
a cited location that itself credits the earlier work{IF COMPANIONS: (such as
a companion lemma)}, is not reported, except when its novelty is overstated
(below).

\paragraph{Flags.}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.5em,font=\normalfont\bfseries]
\item[Attribution gap.] The relationship is same technique, adapted, same
route or same result; the finding is main result, central or supporting; and
the preprint cites none of its earlier works for this content anywhere.
\item[Overstated novelty.] The preprint does cite the earlier work for this
content, but its abstract, a statement of its contributions, or the framing of
a main result presents that content as new, explicitly or in the way a reader
would reasonably take it. This is the one case in which content the preprint
credits is reported.
\end{description}

\paragraph{How to check a claim.}
Locations in the preprint{IF COMPANIONS: and its companions} use its compiled
numbering (sections, equations, theorems, lemmas, propositions) and, where
helpful, page numbers in its PDF. Locations in other sources give the theorem,
equation and printed page of the version listed in the bibliography. Each
finding ends with a short checklist of the passages to put side by side.
