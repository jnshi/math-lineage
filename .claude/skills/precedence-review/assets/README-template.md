# Precedence review: {PREPRINT TITLE}

**Preprint:** [{PREPRINT TITLE}](paper.pdf) ({AUTHOR}, {PREPRINT DATE})

**Write-up:** [precedence-findings.pdf](precedence-findings.pdf) ({REVIEW DATE})

The write-up reports three kinds of finding:
- **(A)** techniques and theory from earlier papers that the preprint reuses without attribution;
- **(B)** earlier, uncited partial progress on the same problem;
- **(C)** a published result that would replace a large part of the argument.

There are {N} findings: {a} Strong and {b} Partial in (A), {c} Partial in (B), and {d} {Major/Moderate} in (C). Of the {W} earlier works involved, {X} are absent from the preprint's bibliography.

| ID | Grade | Technique or result | Earlier work |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Strong | {technique} | {Authors Year; …} |
| P1 | Partial | {technique} | {Authors Year} |
| P2 | Partial (B) | {earlier result} | {Authors Year} |
| C1 | Major (C) | {what becomes a citation} | {Authors Year} |

**Grades:**
- **Strong:** the earlier technique is reused essentially intact without credit.
- **Partial:** only a fragment is reused, or the credit is only indirect.
- **Major / Moderate:** a component of the main theorem or a route becomes a citation, or a multi-page lemma chain does.
