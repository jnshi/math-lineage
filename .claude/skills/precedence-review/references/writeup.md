# The expert write-up

A LaTeX report for experts in the field who have **not read the preprint** and will **only read the PDF**. They must be able to check every claim at a glance. The report has a wider purpose too: to help readers learn the field by seeing what the preprint has in common with earlier work. That purpose is served by **precise correspondences**, not by tutorial prose.

## Structure
```
reviews/<slug>/writeup/
  precedence-findings.tex     main file: title, \input{parts/summary}, ToC, about, overview, three finding sections, bibliography
  parts/preamble.tex          from <skill>/assets/preamble.tex (fixed macros and styling)
  parts/about.tex             from <skill>/assets/about.tex (scope, grades, how to check)
  parts/overview.tex          written by you: the preprint in brief plus all shared notation
  parts/summary.tex           summary table (you write it at assembly)
  parts/bibliography.tex      generated by make_bib.py; never edit by hand
  findings/<ID>-<slug>.tex    one fragment per finding, \subsection{<ID>. <title>}\label{f:<ID>}
  findings/retired/           withdrawn or merged fragments (kept, not input)
  findings/pre-check/         backup before the apply-verdicts pass
  build/                      pdflatex output; build/full.txt is the pdftotext dump used for QA
```
The main file template is `<skill>/assets/precedence-findings.tex`.

## Scaffolding (you, before launching drafters)
1. **Copy `assets/preamble.tex` and `assets/about.tex`.** Fill the placeholders in `about.tex`.
   - It explains the categories, the grades (Strong/Partial, Major/Moderate) and the specific-content rule.
   - Its last paragraph says locators use compiled numbering.
   - Put `% BIB OAI-...: <citation>` lines for the preprint and its companions at its top.
2. **Write `overview.tex` yourself** (`\section{The preprint in brief, and notation}\label{sec:overview}`). It covers the model, the main theorem, the proof routes and every piece of notation used by more than one finding. Drafters reuse it rather than redefining things.
   - Keep it tight.
   - Don't include a section/page map; the preprint has its own table of contents.
3. **Test-compile** it with `bash <skill>/scripts/fragment_test.sh parts/preamble.tex parts/overview.tex <scratch>/overview-test`.
4. **List the findings** from `FINDINGS.md` with their IDs (`S1…`, `P1…`, `C1…`; Strong first, then Partial, then Structural; within each category, reading order or significance) and file names. Pending merges are the user's decision: recommend one and ask.

## Drafting brief
Write the shared part to `<scratch>/writeup-brief.md`. Each agent's prompt is that text followed by a `## YOUR FINDING` block. Launch all drafting agents **in parallel**, in the background.

````text
You are drafting ONE finding for a LaTeX report about a mathematics preprint. The readers are **human experts in the field who have not read the preprint and will only read the PDF**. They must be able to verify each claim at a glance: precise statements, exact compiled locators, and correspondences laid out side by side. The document must be **concise**. Today is {TODAY}.

## Context
- **The preprint:** "{TITLE}" ({DATE}). TeX: `{PREPRINT_DIR}/build/sections/*.tex`; compiled PDF: `{PREPRINT_DIR}/paper.pdf`.
- **Companions** (same release; not prior literature): {LIST WITH PATHS}.
- **Compiled-text extractions with page breaks** (`\f`; the page of a match is 1 plus the number of `\f` before it): `{SCRATCH}/paper.txt` and {COMPANION TEXT FILES}. Find compiled numbers with `python -I -X utf8 "{SKILL}/scripts/pagefind.py" <txt> "<regex>"`.
- **Review materials:** `{REVIEW_DIR}`: `FINDINGS.md` (the digest) and the section reports `NN-*.md`. They hold detailed correspondences and locators **but may contain errors**.
- **Source library:** `{REPO}/prior-work/` (`src/<Key>/` LaTeX where available, `papers/<Key>.pdf`, `text/<Key>.txt`). Grep `INDEX.md` for keys; don't read it whole.
- **The write-up:** `{WRITEUP_DIR}`. Read `parts/overview.tex` (shared notation; don't repeat it) and `parts/about.tex` (scope and grades). {ONCE SOME FRAGMENTS ARE FINISHED: Look at `findings/<a finished fragment>` as a model of the target style and length.}

## Grading (for your understanding)
- **Strong:** an earlier technique is reused essentially intact without credit for that content. New ingredients do not lower the grade.
- **Partial:** only a fragment is reused, or credit is present only indirectly.
- **Indirect credit** counts only when the preprint cites a source or companion *for that specific content* and that location credits the original.
- **(B)**: earlier, uncited partial progress on the same problem. **(C) Structural**: a published result would replace a large part of the argument; graded Major or Moderate.

## Required structure (exactly these paragraph headings, in this order)
```
\subsection{<ID>. <Title naming the technique or result>}\label{f:<ID>}
\paragraph{Summary.}
\paragraph{Setup.}
\paragraph{Role in the argument.}
\paragraph{The earlier technique.}   (for a (B) finding: \paragraph{The earlier result.})
\paragraph{Correspondence.}
\paragraph{What is new in the preprint.}
\paragraph{How to check.}
```
(A structural finding uses: Summary, Setup, Role in the argument, The earlier results, How the earlier results apply, What the route still contributes, How to check.)
- **Summary.** 4–6 sentences: what is reused (or what earlier result precedes) and from where (main locator); where in the preprint (compiled locators); in one sentence, what the preprint does or does not cite for it; the grade (`\grade{Strong}` / `\grade{Partial}`) with a one-clause reason.
- **Setup.** Only the local notation needed, with what each object means. Refer to Section~\ref{sec:overview} for shared notation.
- **Role in the argument.** A few sentences. Recurrences elsewhere go here in one sentence.
- **The earlier technique / result.** Its precise statement in the source's own notation, with locators: just what the reader needs to see the correspondence. **Do not write tutorial explanations.**
- **Correspondence.** One table mapping objects and statements, each side in its own native notation, with compiled locators. Then the one or two key formulas side by side.
- **What is new in the preprint.** A short list.
- **How to check.** An `enumerate` of 2–4 side-by-side checks with exact locations on both sides.

You may present the parts in a different order or organization if that reads better for this finding, as long as all the content is there.

Length: **at most 3 pages**, usually 2–2.5.

## Do not include
- a "citation status" paragraph (one sentence in the Summary suffices);
- **caveats of any kind.** The only test is: *"does this mean the preprint is justified in omitting the citation?"* If a reason passes that test, don't write it as a caveat. Change the grade, or conclude the finding fails, and report that in your final message;
- **process notes:** no grade history ("regraded", "originally Partial"), no mention of reports, the library, agents, a review or check pass, searches, "not consulted", "case-insensitive search", or checks a reader cannot reproduce;
- `[not verified: …]` markers: if you can't verify a claim, leave it out, or pose it neutrally as an open question;
- irrelevant asides, source errata, history that doesn't serve the correspondence, or boxed notes;
- **TeX file names, TeX line numbers or TeX labels in the printed text** (see Locators);
- **the preprint's own reference numbers** ("[29]"). Refer to papers by author names plus `\cite`/`\sloc`;
- numbered table captions ("Table 3"). Say "the table below".

## If the finding doesn't hold
Re-verify every claim against the actual passages. The reports may be wrong; use the correct version and list corrections in your final message.
- If the finding is weaker than its grade, write it with the grade you conclude, and report the change in your final message, not in the fragment.
- If it fails entirely (credit found, correspondence not real, textbook in exactly the form reused, source postdates the preprint, generic calculus), still write the fragment honestly, title it "[Withdrawn] …", and say so at the top of your final message. It will be kept out of the document.
The precise correspondences you write also feed a later check of each finding, so precision about what does and doesn't match is valuable either way.

## Locators
- **Preprint and companion locations:** only via `\ploc{<compiled>}{<file:line>}`. The macro prints **only** the first argument, which must be a compiled section, equation, theorem/lemma/proposition number or page that a PDF reader can find, e.g. `\ploc{Lemma~5.2}{profile-spectral.tex:400--420}`, `\ploc{\S13.3, p.~98}{...}`, `\ploc{AcceptedGap, Lemma~8.1, p.~60}{terminal.tex:...}`. Companion locations use the companion's own numbering and pages. Never write a file name, TeX label or line number anywhere else.
- **Sources:** `\sloc{<Key>}{Thm~2.6, p.~12}` or `\cite{<Key>}`, with `<Key>` exactly as in INDEX.md. The preprint and companions use the `OAI-…` keys defined in `parts/about.tex`.
- **Works not in the library:** key `NL-<Name><Year>`, with one comment line `% BIB NL-<Key>: Authors, Title, Venue (Year), DOI` at the top of the fragment. Put only the citation on that line, never notes about how it was found or whether it was read.
- Never invent citations, numbers, quotes or formulas.

## LaTeX style
- Use only the macros in `parts/preamble.tex`: `\R \E \P \one \D \Lgen \Var \Cov \Ent \KL \Tr \ran \norm{} \ip{}{} \ploc{}{} \sloc{}{} \grade{}`. Define no new global macros.
- **Lists:** plain `itemize`/`enumerate` with **no options**; the global settings handle spacing.
- **Structure:** no `\subsubsection`, no `\fbox`, no `\begingroup\sloppy`. If the title is long, give `\subsection` a short optional title and use `\texorpdfstring` for math in titles.
- **Tables:** `\begin{center}\small\begin{xltabular}{\linewidth}{...} ... \end{xltabular}\end{center}` with booktabs rules and **`\addlinespace` after every row** (not before `\bottomrule`).
- Escape special characters, use `--` for ranges, and no Unicode math. Wide displays: `gather*` or split lines.
- **Edit the fragment only with the Write/Edit tools,** never via sed, heredocs or Python string replacement: backslashes get corrupted.
- **Test-compile:** `bash "{SKILL}/scripts/fragment_test.sh" "{WRITEUP_DIR}/parts/preamble.tex" "{WRITEUP_DIR}/findings/<FILENAME>" "{SCRATCH}/test-<ID>"`. Fix all `!` errors and any overfull box over 10pt; undefined citations and references are expected in this standalone test. The script also greps the PDF text for forbidden strings; fix any hit.

## Output
- Write exactly one file: `{WRITEUP_DIR}/findings/<FILENAME>`. Modify nothing else; don't download anything or edit INDEX.md.
- Final message, under 150 words: the file path and page count; your verdict (grade, or withdrawn) and any change from the assigned grade, with the reason; corrections to the reports; `NL-` entries.
````

**The `## YOUR FINDING` block** gives, for one finding:
- **Header line:** the ID, the assigned grade (plus "(B)" or "structural" where applicable), FILENAME and label.
- **Suggested title.**
- **What it is.** The technique, its earlier source(s) with library keys and main locators, where the preprint uses it (file:line, so the agent can find it fast), and what the preprint does and doesn't cite.
- **Materials.** Which section reports and which finding IDs in them; recurrence locations to mention in one sentence; relevant credit-map entries.
- **Specific checks.** Anything the agent must verify. For example: does a cited source cover it? Is it textbook? For a structural finding, check normalizations, hypotheses, and that the claimed independent route really is independent; grep its sections for references into the other route.
- **Permission to withdraw**, for findings that were always borderline: "Feel free to conclude [Withdrawn] if the evidence supports it."

## Early PDF
**When the first fragment lands, build a preview and send it to the user**, rather than waiting for the whole batch. Make `preview.tex` from the main file, with
```latex
\newcommand{\fragment}[2]{\IfFileExists{findings/#1.tex}{\input{findings/#1}}{\subsection*{#2: draft pending}}}
```
and `\fragment{<file>}{<ID>}` lines. `make_bib.py` follows `\fragment` too. Hold the next batch until the user has reviewed this one. Feedback on the first batch of the first run produced most of the rules in this file, and it is cheaper to catch problems early.

## Processing drafting hand-backs
- **Log each one in a short status line:** the verdict, any grade change, the page count.
- **A downgraded finding:** move it to the Partial section and rename it (an S-file becomes a P-file, with title and label to match).
- **A withdrawn finding:** move the fragment to `findings/retired/`.
- **Report corrections the drafter found:** fold them into `FINDINGS.md`.
- **Two findings overlapping, or "is X just Y twice?":** ask the user. Give numbered options (merge / downgrade / keep separate) and your recommendation. If they choose a merge, one agent rewrites the surviving fragment, integrating each paragraph rather than appending. The cap is about 4 pages. The other fragment goes to `retired/`.

## Assembly
1. **Renumber.** Make IDs contiguous: `python -I -X utf8 <skill>/scripts/renumber_findings.py <writeup dir> S4=S3 S5=S4 P5=P4`. This changes titles, labels, refs, "Finding X" text and file names. Then grep `\label{f:`, `\ref{f:` and `\subsection{` to confirm.
2. **Update the main file's `\input` order:** Strong, then Partial, then Structural.
3. **Write `parts/summary.tex`.** Use `print_summaries.py` to collect each finding's title and Summary.
   - It is an unnumbered "Summary of findings" section with a short lead-in.
   - The table is a `\footnotesize` xltabular with columns **ID | Grade | Technique or result (earlier source) | In the preprint | Credited?**.
   - IDs are `\hyperref[f:<ID>]{<ID>}`. Grades are `\grade{Strong}`, `\grade{Partial}`, `\grade{Partial} (B)` or `\grade{Major} (C)`.
   - Cite the sources in the technique cell.
   - Put `\addlinespace` between rows and `\midrule` between the S, P and C groups.
   - **The Credited? cell is `---` for structural findings,** and the lead-in says the column does not apply to them.
   - End with `\clearpage`. See `assets/summary.tex`.
4. **Build** (see QA gates below).
5. **Look at the PDF.** Use Read with `pages` on the summary table and on one or two correspondence tables, to catch layout problems such as big gaps or crowded rows.
6. **Copy the result.** Copy `build/precedence-findings.pdf` to `writeup/precedence-findings.pdf`, and send that copy with SendUserFile. Before any author-persona agents read it, also dump its text to `build/full.txt`.

## QA gates (every build that goes to the user)
Run `bash <skill>/scripts/build.sh <writeup dir> <repo>/prior-work/INDEX.md precedence-findings.tex`. It:
- regenerates the bibliography;
- runs pdflatex three times;
- prints the missing bib keys, the pdflatex exit status, undefined references, overfull boxes and the page count;
- writes `build/full.txt`;
- greps that text for forbidden strings.

The build passes only if all of these hold:
- there are 0 missing bibliography keys;
- the exit status is 0;
- there are 0 undefined references, and no `??` in the text;
- every overfull box is under 10pt;
- the forbidden-string grep comes back empty. It covers `.tex`, `tex:`, TeX labels, "citation status", "caveat", "not verified", "consulted", "library", "skeptic", "regrad", "case-insensitive", "Table <n>", and bibliography working notes;
- any finding whose claim just changed is checked with a targeted grep for the removed wording.

## Rules for the document, as settled in review of the first run
1. **Order and structure.** Strong, then Partial, then Structural. A summary table at the top. Each finding gives the summary, the setup and notation, its role in the big picture, the earlier mathematics, and a correspondence table in each paper's native notation.
2. **The document is LaTeX.**
3. **Drafters may reorder** a finding's parts.
4. **No calibration section** ("examined and found credited, original, or out of scope"). It would just be the rest of the paper.
5. **An early PDF** goes out after the first fragment.
6. **The write-up comes before the author-persona check,** because its correspondences feed that check. Drafters may conclude that a finding fails.
7. **No process notes and no grade history** in the document.
8. **No TeX file:line locators.** Use compiled numbering only.
9. **Concision.**
   - No "citation status" sections.
   - No caveats (apply the test above).
   - No section-page map.
   - Uniform list and table styling, with space between table rows.
   - No irrelevant asides.
10. **Don't expand explanations of earlier techniques.** Agents don't write good explanations for learners. The contextualizing purpose is served by precise correspondences.
11. **The Credited? column does not apply to category C.**
