\section{About this document}\label{sec:about}

\paragraph{The preprint.}
The preprint is \emph{A spectral gap throughout the high-temperature
Sherrington--Kirkpatrick phase}, dated September~24, 2026, with author line
``OpenAI'' \cite{OAI-SpectralGap}. It is one of 719 manuscripts in the
\texttt{openai/math} release, all produced by an unreleased internal OpenAI
model. It proves that for every fixed inverse temperature $0<\beta<1$, the
zero-field Gaussian SK Gibbs measure satisfies, with probability tending to one
over the disorder, a Poincar\'e inequality for the unscaled heat-bath
Dirichlet form with a constant depending only on $\beta$.
Section~\ref{sec:overview} summarizes the paper and fixes the notation used
throughout this document. The preprint cites no other preprint from the same
release.

\paragraph{What counts as a finding.}
This document reports only:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.5em,font=\normalfont\bfseries]
\item[(A) Unattributed reuse.] A technique, construction, identity, estimate or
body of theory developed in an identifiable earlier paper is reused without
attribution: either nothing is cited, or the citation given covers something
else or something narrower than what is reused.
\item[(B) Same-problem precedence.] Earlier, uncited partial progress on the
same problem, such as a weaker or special-case version of a statement the
preprint proves.
\item[(C) Structural.] A published result would replace or substantially
shorten a large part of the argument, or contradicts a claim the preprint
makes about why part of the argument is necessary.
\end{description}
Textbook tools and ``should cite the original rather than a later source''
quibbles are not reported.

\paragraph{Grades.}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.5em,font=\normalfont\bfseries]
\item[\grade{Strong}.] The earlier technique is reused essentially intact
without credit for that content. This holds even when the preprint combines it
with new ingredients or applies it in a new setting. New ingredients are
listed as differences and do not lower the grade.
\item[\grade{Partial}.] Only a fragment of the earlier technique is reused, or
the credit is present but only indirectly.
\end{description}
Same-problem findings (B) are graded by how close the earlier result comes to
the preprint's: \grade{Strong} when it proves essentially the same statement,
\grade{Partial} when it is a weaker, special-case or partial-range version.
Structural findings (C) are graded by impact instead:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.5em,font=\normalfont\bfseries]
\item[\grade{Major}.] A route, a section, or a component of the main theorem
becomes a citation.
\item[\grade{Moderate}.] A multi-page chain of lemmas becomes a citation.
\end{description}
Grades measure how completely the \emph{earlier technique} is reused, not how
much of the preprint's argument it accounts for. Credit counts as indirect
only when the preprint cites a source \emph{for that specific content}, and
the cited location itself covers or credits the original. Citing the same
paper nearby for a different result does not count. This matters because the
preprint states that ``we prove the model-specific estimates in the sections
below'' (\S1.2, p.~5), and most of its later sections contain no citations.

\section{The preprint in brief, and notation}\label{sec:overview}

\paragraph{Model and theorem.}
Fix $0<\beta<1$ and write $j=\beta^2$. The disorder $J$ is a symmetric
$n\times n$ matrix with zero diagonal and independent entries
$J_{ik}\sim N(0,j/n)$ for $i<k$. For a field $h\in\R^n$, the Gibbs law on
$\{-1,1\}^n$ is
\[
 \mu_h(x)\propto\exp\Bigl\{\tfrac12 x^{\mathsf T}Jx+h^{\mathsf T}x\Bigr\},
\]
so $\mu_0$ is the zero-field SK model. With $P_i^h$ the conditional
expectation under $\mu_h$ given all spins but the $i$th, the unscaled
heat-bath Dirichlet form is
$\D_h(f)=\sum_{i}\E_{\mu_h}(f-P_i^hf)^2$
\ploc{(1.1)}{introduction.tex:22--24}. The main result
\ploc{(Theorem~1.1)}{introduction.tex:33--42} is that, with probability
tending to one over $J$, $\Var_{\mu_0}(f)\le C_\beta\D_0(f)$ for every
$f:\{-1,1\}^n\to\R$. The conditional mean and variance of $x_i$ under
$\mu_{h}$ are $m^1_i=\tanh h^1_i$ and $v^1_i=1-(m^1_i)^2$, where
$h^1=h+Jx$ is the local field \ploc{(\S1.2)}{introduction.tex:152--161}.
For a vector $v$, $\langle v\rangle=n^{-1}\sum_iv_i$.

\paragraph{Route of the proof.}
A spin sample is observed in Gaussian noise,
\[
 X\sim\mu_0,\qquad Y(t)=tX+B(t),\qquad 0\le t\le T,
\]
with $B$ a standard Brownian motion \ploc{(1.4)}{introduction.tex:198--201};
given the observations up to time $t$, the spin law is $\mu_{Y(t)}$. This is
stochastic localization in its Gaussian-channel form.
Section~2 of the preprint transfers three inputs back to $\mu_0$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item stability of the TAP field equation at every field visited by the
observation path, except on an event of probability $e^{-an}$
\ploc{(Proposition~5.1, built on \S\S3--5)}{stability.tex:35--60};
\item at such stable fields, a directional covariance bound
$\norm{\Cov_{\mu_h}(G,x)}^2\le C(\Var_{\mu_h}(G)+\D_h(G))$
\ploc{(Proposition~7.2)}{consequences.tex:89--98} and a bound on the mean of
$x$ under $f^2\mu_h$ \ploc{(Proposition~7.3)}{consequences.tex:198--243},
both obtained from finite recursions and residual identities
\ploc{(\S6)}{residuals.tex};
\item a terminal gap $\Var_{\mu_{Y(T)}}f\le2\D_{Y(T)}(f)$ at a large fixed
time $T$ \ploc{(Proposition~8.3)}{terminal.tex:334--346}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The field map and stability.}
Fields are written in the coordinates $y=\operatorname{arctanh}m$. Put
$m(y)=\tanh y$, $q(y)=\langle m(y)^2\rangle$, $b(y)=1-q(y)$ and
$V_y=\operatorname{diag}(1-m(y)^2)$. The field map
\ploc{(5.1)}{stability.tex:10--19} is
\[
 F_{J,h_0}(y)=y-h_0+\bigl(jb(y)I-J\bigr)m(y),
\]
whose zeros are the TAP equations with Onsager term $jb\,m$. For a
nonnegative diagonal matrix $A$, the stability matrix
\ploc{(5.2)}{stability.tex:24--28} is
\[
 \mathsf H_J(y,A)=I+A^{1/2}\Bigl(jb(y)I-J-\tfrac{2j}{n}m(y)m(y)^{\mathsf T}\Bigr)A^{1/2};
\]
at $A=V_y$ it is $V_y^{1/2}\,\mathcal H(m)\,V_y^{1/2}$, where
$\mathcal H(m)=\operatorname{diag}(1/(1-m^2))+j(1-q)I-J-\tfrac{2j}{n}mm^{\mathsf T}$
is the Hessian of the negative TAP free energy. ``Stable'' means that every
approximate zero, $\norm{F_{J,h_0}(y)}\le2\rho_0\sqrt n$, has
$\mathsf H_J(y,A)\succ cI$ for all diagonal $A$ near $V_y$
\ploc{(5.3)}{stability.tex:45--56}.

\paragraph{The stability integral.}
Stability is proved under the planted law
\ploc{(5.4)}{stability.tex:112--116}
\[
 \widetilde J=W+\tfrac jn\one\one^{\mathsf T},\qquad h_0=t\one+\sqrt t\,g,
\]
with $W$ a GOE matrix of scale $j$ and $g$ standard Gaussian, using the
smoothed integral \ploc{(5.5)}{stability.tex:120--127}
\[
 I_{t,\sigma}(\mathcal E)=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-n/2}\int
 \one_{\{(y,\widetilde J)\in\mathcal E\}}
 \exp\Bigl\{-\frac{\norm{F_{\widetilde J,h_0}(y)}^2}{2\sigma^2}
 +\frac{njb(y)^2}{2}\Bigr\}\,dy .
\]
Its exponential rate is a function of the empirical law $P$ of the
coordinates of $y$: with $M=\E_P\tanh$, $q=\E_P\tanh^2$, $b=1-q$,
$d=t+jM$, $s=t+\sigma^2+jq$, $S=s+jq$ and $a=\E_P[(y-d)\tanh y]$, the rate is
$\psi-\KL(P\Vert N(d,s))$ with $\psi=j(a-sb)^2/(2sS)$
\ploc{(5.6)--(5.9)}{stability.tex:136--224}. The preprint's scalar entropy
inequality \ploc{(Lemma~4.1)}{entropy.tex:16--30} states
$\KL(P\Vert N(d,s))\ge\psi$, with equality only at $P=N(d,s)$; the relevant
equality law is $N(s_*(t),s_*(t))$, where $s_*=t+j\,\E_{N(s_*,s_*)}\tanh$
\ploc{(Lemma~4.2)}{entropy.tex:220--243}.

\paragraph{The recursion.}
At a field $h_0$, starting from a spin sample $x\sim\mu_{h_0}$, the preprint
runs \ploc{(6.1)}{residuals.tex:36--43}
\[
 m^0=x,\quad b_0=0,\quad m^l=\tanh h^l,\quad b_l=\langle1-(m^l)^2\rangle,\quad
 h^{l+1}=h_0+Jm^l-jb_lm^{l-1},
\]
with residuals $R_l=m^{l-1}-m^l$, through a fixed finite depth. Spin-flip
derivatives are $d_if(x)=(f(x)-f(x^{(i)}))/(2x_i)$, where $x^{(i)}$ flips
the $i$th spin, and $\norm G_*^2=\E_{\mu_{h_0}}G^2+\D_{h_0}(G)$
\ploc{(\S1.2)}{introduction.tex:143--161}.

\paragraph{Summary.}
The stability proposition of the preprint is proved with the Kac--Rice
computation of Fan, Mei and Montanari (``FMM'')
\cite{FanMeiMontanari2018-TAPFreeEnergyVariationalInference} for critical
points of the TAP free energy in planted SK: the gauge $x=\one$, the TAP
gradient, its density from $\Cov(Wm)$, the determinant rate
\sloc{FanMeiMontanari2018-TAPFreeEnergyVariationalInference}{Prop.~3.2, (3.5), p.~9},
and the relative-entropy rate
\sloc{FanMeiMontanari2018-TAPFreeEnergyVariationalInference}{Prop.~3.5 and \S3.4, pp.~10--11},
which vanishes at the Bayes point
\sloc{FanMeiMontanari2018-TAPFreeEnergyVariationalInference}{Prop.~5.2, p.~22}.
As Celentano, Fan and Mei (``CFM'') do
\sloc{CelentanoFanMei2021-LocalConvexityTAPZ2Sync}{Lemmas~4.7--4.9, Cor.~4.10, pp.~17--18},
it restricts the count to Hessian failures near the Bayes law, and it uses
CFM's Sudakov--Fernique comparison
\sloc{CelentanoFanMei2021-LocalConvexityTAPZ2Sync}{Lemma~4.1, p.~15} (which
CFM apply to the smallest eigenvalue of the TAP Hessian) as its bulk input. In the preprint this is
\ploc{\S5.1--\S5.2 and the bound (5.25) (pp.~28--37)}{stability.tex:107--700, 864--906},
with \ploc{Lemma~3.5 (p.~20)}{matrices.tex:721--741} for the determinant and
\ploc{Lemma~3.2 (p.~12)}{matrices.tex:145--236} for the comparison; at
$t=\sigma=0$ its scalar rate is, term by term, FMM's variational functional
of $\rho$ from \S3.4, whose constrained supremum is their complexity.
Neither FMM nor CFM is cited, and the words Kac, Rice and complexity do
not occur. The only citation on TAP-Hessian positivity is Celentano
\cite{Celentano2022-SudakovFerniquePostAMPTAPConvexity}, for local convexity
of the El Alaoui--Montanari--Sellke TAP free energy near late AMP iterates
\ploc{(\S1.1, p.~4)}{introduction.tex:118--125}; that paper proves this
without the Kac--Rice formula and credits CFM for the analogous result for
the FMM free energy and for key steps
\sloc{Celentano2022-SudakovFerniquePostAMPTAPConvexity}{pp.~4--5}. Lemma~3.2
presents the comparison as a standard Gaussian fact with only Vitale cited. Grade: \grade{Strong}, because FMM's computation is
reproduced at the level of equations and CFM's restricted count at the level
of statements, without credit.

\paragraph{Setup.}
Section~\ref{sec:overview} defines $F$, $\mathsf H_J(y,A)$, $V_y$, the
planted law (5.4), the integral $I_{t,\sigma}$ (5.5) and the scalar
parameters $M,q,b,d,s,S,a,\psi$ (we write $a$ for the preprint's
$a_y=\langle(y-d\one)m\rangle$). The finding also uses:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Gaussian reduction}
\ploc{(Lemma~5.3, (5.8), pp.~28--29)}{stability.tex:157--203}. With
$z=y-d\one+jbm$ and $\varphi_{d,s}$ the $N(d,s)$ density,
\[
 \E I_{t,\sigma}(\mathcal E)=\int\sqrt{s/S}\,\prod_i\varphi_{d,s}(y_i)\,e^{n\psi}\,
 \P\bigl((y,\widetilde J)\in\mathcal E\,\big|\,Wm+\sqrt{t+\sigma^2}\,g'=z\bigr)\,dy .
\]
By Lemma~4.1 the unrestricted rate $\psi-\KL(P\Vert N(d,s))$ is $\le0$
\ploc{(5.9), p.~29}{stability.tex:205--224}; by
\ploc{Lemma~4.2}{entropy.tex:220--243}, equality at $\sigma=0$ holds only on
the curve $P_t=N(s_*(t),s_*(t))$.
\item \emph{Witness set} \ploc{(\S5.4, p.~37)}{stability.tex:881--890}: for
$t\ge t_0$, $E_t(W)$ is the set of $y$ for which some diagonal
$0\le A\le(1+\eta)I$ with $\norm{A-V_y}_{\mathrm F}\le\epsilon_*\sqrt n$ has
$\lambda_{\min}(\mathsf H_{\widetilde J}(y,A))\le c_E$.
\item \emph{Determinant} \ploc{(Lemma~3.5, (3.26), p.~20)}{matrices.tex:721--741}:
for each fixed $0<\gamma\le\gamma_0(j,\varepsilon)$, with probability
$1-Ce^{-cn}$, simultaneously over diagonal $0\le V\le I$ and $E$ of bounded
rank and norm, $L=I+(jbI-W)V+E$ with
$b=n^{-1}\Tr V$ has $n^{-1}\log\det(L^{\mathsf T}L+\gamma I)\le jb^2+\varepsilon$;
it is applied to $L=F'_{\widetilde J,h_0}(y)$
\ploc{(p.~38)}{stability.tex:938--957}.
\item \emph{Bulk comparison}
\ploc{(Lemma~3.2, (3.8), p.~12)}{matrices.tex:145--159}: for deterministic
diagonal $0\le A\le a_+I$, $ja_+^2<1$, $\chi_A=n^{-1}\Tr A$,
$S_A(\tau;W)=I+\tau^2j\chi_AA-\tau A^{1/2}WA^{1/2}\succeq c_0I$ for all
$\tau\in[0,1]$ with probability $1-Ce^{-cn}$. At $\tau=1$, $A=V_y$ it is
$V_y^{1/2}(\cdot)V_y^{1/2}$ of the TAP Hessian minus its rank-one terms.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Role in the argument.}
\ploc{Proposition~5.1 (p.~27)}{stability.tex:35--60}, input~1 in
Section~\ref{sec:overview}, says that along the observation path every
approximate zero of $F$ has $\mathsf H_J(y,A)\succ cI$ for all nearby $A$.
Its proof rests on $\E I_{t,\sigma}(E_t)\le e^{-a_0n}$
\ploc{(5.25), p.~37}{stability.tex:891--906}, which is the Kac--Rice count
of this finding: the scalar rate excludes everything outside a neighborhood
of $P_t$ \ploc{(Lemma~5.4, pp.~29--31)}{stability.tex:226--389}, and near
$P_t$ the conditional Hessian fails with probability at most $e^{-a_*n}$
\ploc{(Lemma~5.5, pp.~31--35)}{stability.tex:399--700}. Passing from (5.25)
to approximate zeros (Gaussian smoothing and the local-volume
Lemma~5.6) is finding~\ref{f:huang-sellke-approx-crit}, where Lemma~3.5's
rate cancels the weight $e^{njb^2/2}$; the planted law and its transfer
are finding~\ref{f:eams-framework}.

\paragraph{The earlier technique.}
FMM and CFM study $Y=(\lambda/n)xx^{\mathsf T}+W_{\mathrm F}$ with
$x\in\{\pm1\}^n$ and $W_{\mathrm F}$ a GOE matrix with off-diagonal variance
$1/n$, and the TAP free energy at inverse temperature $\beta$; CFM take the
Bayes (Nishimori) case $\beta=\lambda>1$. Write
$Q=\norm m^2/n$ and $M=\ip{\one}{m}/n$. We denote FMM's
scalar auxiliary variable (their $y$) by $\vartheta$, their
$A(\rho)=\int x\operatorname{arctanh}x\,\rho(dx)$ by $a_{\mathrm F}$, and
their $L(m)$ by $L_{\mathrm F}(m)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Gauge and derivatives}
\sloc{FanMeiMontanari2018-TAPFreeEnergyVariationalInference}{\S3, (3.2)--(3.3), p.~8}:
by sign symmetry $x=\one$, and
\begin{align*}
 g_n(m)&=\operatorname{arctanh}(m)-\tfrac{\beta\lambda}{n}\ip{\one}{m}\one-\beta W_{\mathrm F}m+\beta^2(1-Q)m,\\
 H_n(m)&=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(1/(1-m^2)\bigr)-\tfrac{\beta\lambda}{n}\one\one^{\mathsf T}-\beta W_{\mathrm F}+\beta^2(1-Q)I-\tfrac{2\beta^2}{n}mm^{\mathsf T}.
\end{align*}
\item \emph{Kac--Rice integrand.}
$\E\,\mathrm{Crit}_n\le\int\E\bigl[|\det H_n(m)|\,\big|\,g_n(m)=0\bigr]p_m(0)\,dm$
\sloc{FanMeiMontanari2018-TAPFreeEnergyVariationalInference}{Prop.~3.1, (3.4), p.~9},
with
$\E[|\det H_n(m)|\mid g_n(m)=0]\le e^{nL_{\mathrm F}(m)+o(n)}$,
$L_{\mathrm F}(m)=\tfrac{\beta^2}{2}(1-Q)^2+\tfrac1n\sum_i\log\tfrac1{1-m_i^2}$
\sloc{FanMeiMontanari2018-TAPFreeEnergyVariationalInference}{Prop.~3.2, (3.5), p.~9},
proved in \S4 by free convolution. The gradient density uses
$W_{\mathrm F}m\overset d=Q^{1/2}v+\beta^{-1}\vartheta m$ with
independent $v\sim N(0,I_n)$ and $\vartheta\sim N(0,\beta^2/n)$; $p_m(0)$
integrates over $\vartheta$
\sloc{FanMeiMontanari2018-TAPFreeEnergyVariationalInference}{Lemma~3.3, p.~9}.
\item \emph{Rate.} Sanov and Varadhan give
\[
 \limsup_n\tfrac1n\log\E\,\mathrm{Crit}_n\le\sup_\rho\sup_\vartheta
 \bigl\{J(\rho,\vartheta)-H(\rho|\pi_0)-\vartheta^2/(2\beta^2)+\log2\bigr\},
\]
where $J$ collects $L_{\mathrm F}$ and the density of Lemma~3.3
\sloc{FanMeiMontanari2018-TAPFreeEnergyVariationalInference}{Cor.~3.4, (3.6), pp.~9--10; Prop.~3.5, p.~10; App.~B, pp.~32--35}.
The supremum over $\vartheta$ is computed in closed form
\sloc{FanMeiMontanari2018-TAPFreeEnergyVariationalInference}{\S3.4, p.~11}:
\begin{equation*}
 \sup_\vartheta\bigl[\cdots\bigr]
 =\frac1{4\beta^2}\Bigl(\beta^2(1-q)+\frac{\beta\lambda\varphi^2}{q}-\frac{a_{\mathrm F}}{q}\Bigr)^2
 -H(\rho\,|\,\rho_{\varphi,q}),
\end{equation*}
with $(q,\varphi,a_{\mathrm F})$ the moments of $\rho$ and $\rho_{\varphi,q}$
(density $h_{\varphi,q}$) the image of $N(\beta\lambda\varphi,\beta^2q)$
under $\tanh$.
\item \emph{Bayes point}
\sloc{FanMeiMontanari2018-TAPFreeEnergyVariationalInference}{Prop.~5.2, p.~22; proof p.~24}:
the complexity $S_\star$ vanishes at $(q_\star,\varphi_\star,a_\star,e_\star)$,
using $\xi\sim N(\lambda^2q_\star,\lambda^2q_\star)$, the identity
$\E\tanh\xi=\E\tanh^2\xi$ and $a_\star=\E\,\xi\tanh\xi$; for
$\lambda\ge\lambda_0$,
$S_\star\le-c(\lambda)[(a-a_\star)^2+(q-q_\star)^2+(\varphi-\varphi_\star)^2+(e-e_\star)^2]$
whenever $|q-q_\star|,|\varphi-\varphi_\star|\le c_0/\lambda^2$ ($a,e$ free).
\end{enumerate}
CFM restrict the count to Hessian failures near the Bayes law, with
$g_n,H_n$ at $\beta=\lambda$ and a threshold $t_{\mathrm H}$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Localization}
\sloc{CelentanoFanMei2021-LocalConvexityTAPZ2Sync}{Lemma~4.7, p.~17; proof pp.~41--43}:
critical points with overlaps, entropy and free energy near the Bayes values lie in
$\mathcal D_\eta$, where the empirical law of $\operatorname{arctanh}m_i$ is
$W_2$-within $\eta$ of $N(\lambda^2q_\star,\lambda^2q_\star)$; the proof
reduces to that product density and a $W_2$ deviation bound.
\item \emph{Restricted Kac--Rice}
\sloc{CelentanoFanMei2021-LocalConvexityTAPZ2Sync}{Lemma~4.8, p.~17}: for
$\mathcal C=\{m\in T:g_n(m)=0,\ \ell(m,W)+U<t_{\mathrm H}\}$ with
$U\sim\mathrm{Unif}[-c,c]$,
$\E|\mathcal C|\le\int_T\E\bigl[|\det H_n(m)|\,\one\{\ell(m,W)+U<t_{\mathrm H}\}\,\big|\,g_n(m)=0\bigr]p_{g_n(m)}(0)\,dm$.
\item \emph{Conditional Hessian}
\sloc{CelentanoFanMei2021-LocalConvexityTAPZ2Sync}{Lemma~4.9, p.~18; Lemma~B.2, p.~43}:
with
$\ell_\varepsilon^+(m)=\inf\{\lambda_{\min}H_n(u):\norm{u-m}\le\sqrt{\varepsilon n}\}$,
every $m\in\mathcal D_\eta$ has
$\P[\ell_\varepsilon^+(m)<3t_{\mathrm H}\mid g_n(m)=0]\le e^{-c_1n}$
\sloc{CelentanoFanMei2021-LocalConvexityTAPZ2Sync}{proof of Cor.~4.10, p.~50}. The lower bound on
its conditional mean comes from the comparison
\sloc{CelentanoFanMei2021-LocalConvexityTAPZ2Sync}{Lemma~4.1, p.~15; proof App.~A.4, p.~33}
\[
 \E\sup_x\bigl[v(x)^{\mathsf T}W_{\mathrm F}v(x)+f(x)\bigr]
 \le\E\sup_x\Bigl[\tfrac2{\sqrt n}\norm{v(x)}\ip{\zeta}{v(x)}+f(x)\Bigr],
 \qquad\zeta\sim N(0,I_n),
\]
with the TAP diagonal and Onsager term in $f$.
\item \emph{Assembly}
\sloc{CelentanoFanMei2021-LocalConvexityTAPZ2Sync}{proof of Cor.~4.10, p.~50}:
by Cauchy--Schwarz the restricted count is at most
$\int_{\mathcal D_\eta}\E[|\det H_n|^2\mid g_n=0]^{1/2}p(0)\,dm\le e^{c_1n/4}$
(Lemma~B.3, p.~49, from FMM's zero at the Bayes point) times
$\sup_m\P[\ell_\varepsilon^+<3t_{\mathrm H}\mid g_n=0]^{1/2}\le e^{-c_1n/2}$.
\end{itemize}

factors as $L=H_n(m)V_y$, and $dm=\prod_i(1-m_i^2)\,dy$, so
\[
 \det L=\det H_n(m)\prod_i(1-m_i^2),\qquad

 =e^{njb^2/2}\prod_i\frac1{1-m_i^2}\cdot\prod_i(1-m_i^2)\,dy
 =e^{njb^2/2}\,dy ,
\]
and Lemma~3.5's bound $\frac1{2n}\log\det(L^{\mathsf T}L+\gamma I)\le\frac{jb^2}2+\frac\varepsilon2$

\finding{Approximate-critical-point control from annealed Kac--Rice bounds}{huang-sellke-approx-crit}

\paragraph{Summary.}
To exclude unstable \emph{approximate} solutions of the TAP field equation,
the preprint uses the device of Huang and Sellke (HS)
\cite{HuangSellke2023-StrongTopologicalTrivialization} that turns an
exponentially small annealed Kac--Rice bound on exact critical points with
a bad property into high-probability non-existence of approximate ones
\sloc{HuangSellke2023-StrongTopologicalTrivialization}{\S5: Theorem~5.2, p.~28; Lemma~5.11, pp.~33--35; proof of Theorem~5.2, p.~36}.
The device has four parts: a Gaussian rerandomization, so that approximate
critical points contribute to a Kac--Rice integral; a local lower bound in
which the inverse-Jacobian volume of a region adapted to the Jacobian
cancels the determinant factor; inheritance of the bad property on that
region; and Markov's inequality against the annealed bound. In the preprint
these are the smoothed integral \ploc{(5.5)}{stability.tex:119--134} with
the convolution in \ploc{the proof of Lemma~5.3 (p.~28)}{stability.tex:179--182},
\ploc{Lemma~5.6 (pp.~35--37)}{stability.tex:713--832}, and
\ploc{the exclusion step in the proof of Proposition~5.1 (pp.~37--38)}{stability.tex:908--969}.
The preprint does not cite HS and presents the step as its own
\ploc{(\S1.1, p.~4; \S1.3, p.~5)}{introduction.tex:122--125; 191--194};
Sellke appears in its bibliography only as a coauthor of
El~Alaoui--Montanari--Sellke
\cite{ElAlaouiMontanariSellke2022-SamplingSKAlgorithmicSL}, cited for
Gaussian observations, planted laws and AMP, which contains no such step. Grade: \grade{Strong}, because all
four parts reappear for HS's purpose: ruling out approximate critical
points with an atypical Hessian.

\paragraph{Setup.}
Section~\ref{sec:overview} defines $F=F_{\widetilde J,h_0}$,
$\mathsf H_J(y,A)$, the planted law (5.4) and $I_{t,\sigma}$ (5.5). This
finding also needs:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Jacobian.} $L=F'(y)=I+(jbI-\widetilde J)V_y-\tfrac{2j}{n}mm^{\mathsf T}V_y$
(not symmetric) and $Q=L^{\mathsf T}L+\gamma I$, $\gamma>0$ fixed
\ploc{(Lemma~5.6)}{stability.tex:719--721}.
\item \emph{Bad set and annealed bound.} For $t\ge t_0$, $E_t(W)$ is the set
of $y$ for which some diagonal $0\le A\le(1+\eta)I$ with
$\norm{A-V_y}_{\mathrm F}\le\epsilon_*\sqrt n$ has
$\lambda_{\min}(\mathsf H_{\widetilde J}(y,A))\le c_E$; for $t<t_0$,
$E_t=\{q(y)\ge q_0\}$ \ploc{(p.~37)}{stability.tex:883--890}. The Kac--Rice
computation of finding~\ref{f:fmm-cfm-kac-rice} gives
$\E I_{t,\sigma}(E_t)\le e^{-a_0n}$ for $0\le t\le T$, $0<\sigma\le\sigma_*$
\ploc{(5.25)}{stability.tex:891--897}.
\item \emph{Witness} \ploc{(5.26)}{stability.tex:911--916}:
$\norm{F(y)}\le\rho\sqrt n$, $\norm{A-V_y}_{\mathrm F}\le\frac12\epsilon_*\sqrt n$
and $\lambda_{\min}(\mathsf H_{\widetilde J}(y,A))\le\frac12c_E$.
\item \emph{Determinant event}
\ploc{(Lemma~3.5, p.~20, applied on p.~38)}{matrices.tex:721--741; stability.tex:948--955}:
with probability at least $1-Ce^{-a_1n}$, simultaneously for all $y$,
$n^{-1}\log\det Q\le jb(y)^2+\varepsilon_1$.
\item \emph{Reading of (5.5).} For a standard Gaussian $\tilde g$,
$F_{\widetilde J,h_0+\sigma\tilde g}=F_{\widetilde J,h_0}-\sigma\tilde g$, so
the area formula gives
\[
 \E_{\tilde g}\#\{y\in E:F_{\widetilde J,h_0+\sigma\tilde g}(y)=0\}
 =(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-n/2}\int_E e^{-\norm{F(y)}^2/(2\sigma^2)}\,|\det L(y)|\,dy .
\]
So $I_{t,\sigma}(E)$ is the right-hand side with $|\det L(y)|$ replaced by
the weight $e^{njb(y)^2/2}$, calibrated to its exponential scale
\ploc{(p.~28)}{stability.tex:129--131}: the smoothed, weighted count
$\E_{\tilde g}\sum e^{njb(y)^2/2}/|\det L(y)|$ over the zeros $y\in E$ of the
rerandomized $F_{\widetilde J,h_0+\sigma\tilde g}$. In the proof of
Lemma~5.3 the rerandomization turns $\sqrt t\,g$ into
$\sqrt{t+\sigma^2}\,g'$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Role in the argument.}
Proposition~5.1 must hold at every approximate zero
($\norm F\le2\rho_0\sqrt n$), not only at exact zeros:
\ploc{Lemma~5.7 (p.~40)}{stability.tex:1105--1207} turns positivity on the
whole small-residual region into a unique zero, within $C\norm{F(y)}$ of

\section{Partial findings}\label{sec:partial}
\findinggroup{P}
\finding[Stein equation for tanh under a cosh-tilted Gaussian]{The Stein equation for \texorpdfstring{$\tanh$}{tanh} under a cosh-tilted Gaussian, used to compare a field with an Onsager-corrected field}{chatterjee-stein}

\paragraph{Summary.}
Chatterjee \cite{Chatterjee2007-SpinGlassesSteinsMethod} derives the TAP
equations by Stein's method for the cosh-tilted Gaussian laws
$\psi\propto\cosh(ax+b)e^{-(x-\mu)^2/2\sigma^2}$: a Stein operator whose
equation for $u(x)=\tanh(ax+b)$ has right side $\tanh(ax+b)-\tanh(a\mu+b)$,
its bounded solution, and its use at the Gibbs local field, centred at the
Onsager-corrected field, with the Onsager coefficient as variance parameter
(in the units $z=\beta\ell+h$)
\sloc{Chatterjee2007-SpinGlassesSteinsMethod}{\S1.8, (12)--(15), p.~7; Lemma~1.3, p.~8; \S3.4, pp.~20--23; Corollary~1.2, p.~5}.
The preprint's scalar identity
\ploc{(6.8)--(6.9), p.~45}{residuals.tex:245--279} is this Stein equation,
with the solution in closed form. It drives the induction step of the weak
residual rule \ploc{(proof of Lemma~6.5, p.~54)}{residuals.tex:966--1004},
where at $p=1$ it compares the Gibbs-sample local field with an
Onsager-corrected field, and it gives the comparison with the TAP root behind
Proposition~7.2 \ploc{((6.12)--(6.13), pp.~45--46; (7.3), p.~57)}{residuals.tex:316--342; consequences.tex:113--131}.
The preprint does not cite Chatterjee; it introduces (6.9) as ``a scalar
identity valid for arbitrary real fields'' and cites Bolthausen
\cite{Bolthausen2012-IterativeTAPConstruction} only for the Onsager-corrected
form of the recursion \ploc{(\S1.1, p.~4; \S6.1, p.~42)}{introduction.tex:125--128; residuals.tex:32--34}.
Grade: \grade{Partial}, because the operator, its bounded solution and the
field--centre--parameter configuration are reused; Chatterjee's $L^2$ estimate
of $\langle Tf\rangle$, the main work of his method, is not.

\paragraph{Setup.}
Section~\ref{sec:overview} defines $J$, $j=\beta^2$, $\mu_{h_0}$, the recursion
(6.1) ($m^l,b_l,h^l,R_l$), $F_{J,h_0}$, $d_i$ and $\norm\cdot_*$. In (6.1) the
field $h^{l+1}$ carries the Onsager term $-jb_lm^{l-1}$; $h^1=h_0+Jx$ carries
none. This finding also needs:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Kernel} \ploc{(6.8)}{residuals.tex:250--253}: for $z,r_0,a\in\R$,
$\Phi(z;r_0,a)=\int_0^1e^{a(1-u^2)/2}\cosh(u(z-r_0))\,du/(\cosh z\cosh r_0)$.
\item \emph{Tests} \ploc{(Definition~6.2, p.~44)}{residuals.tex:179--222}:
vectors $U$ built from the primary fields by finite recipes. The site partial
$\partial_l$ differentiates the explicit argument $h_i^l$ with all else fixed;
``admissible from level $p+1$'' means every explicit primary argument has
index at least $p+1$, so $\partial_pU=0$. Auxiliary fields carry
an Onsager correction, $y=Jw-j\sum_ln^{-1}\bigl(\sum_i\partial_lw_i\bigr)m^{l-1}-\cdots$
\ploc{(6.7)}{residuals.tex:199--205}.

$\E_{\mu_{h_0}}[x_i\mid x_{-i}]=m^1_i=\tanh h^1_i$ and $v^1_i=1-(m^1_i)^2$,
$\E\sum_i(x_i-m_i^1)GU_i=\E\sum_iv_i^1d_i(GU_i)$

\paragraph{The earlier technique.}
Chatterjee's Gibbs measure is
$G_N(\sigma)\propto\exp\{\beta N^{-1/2}\sum_{i<j}g_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_j+h\sum_i\sigma_i\}$,
and $\langle\cdot\rangle$ there is the Gibbs average.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Fields} \sloc{Chatterjee2007-SpinGlassesSteinsMethod}{(5), p.~3; (10), p.~5}:
the local field $\ell_i=N^{-1/2}\sum_{j\ne i}g_{ij}\sigma_j$ and the
Onsager-corrected centre
$r_i=N^{-1/2}\sum_{j\ne i}g_{ij}\langle\sigma_j\rangle-\beta(1-q)\langle\sigma_i\rangle$,
so that $\beta r_i+h$ is the argument of $\tanh$ in the TAP equations.
\item \emph{Stein's method for mixture Gaussians}
\sloc{Chatterjee2007-SpinGlassesSteinsMethod}{\S1.8, p.~7}: $M(a,b,\mu,\sigma^2)$
has density $\psi\propto\cosh(ax+b)e^{-(x-\mu)^2/2\sigma^2}$ (12), a mixture of
$N(\mu\pm a\sigma^2,\sigma^2)$; $\int\tanh(ax+b)\psi=\tanh(a\mu+b)$ (14); the
operator $Tf=f'-\bigl(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma^2}-a\tanh(ax+b)\bigr)f$ characterizes
$M$; and the Stein equation is $Tf=u-\int u\psi$ (15). Lemma~1.3 (p.~8) gives
a solution $f(x,\mu)$ with $|f|,|\partial_xf|,|\partial_\mu f|\le C\norm u_\infty$
uniformly in $(x,\mu)$, namely
$f=\rho^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^x\rho(t,\mu)(u(t)-\int u\psi)\,dt$ with
$\rho=\cosh(ax+b)e^{-(x-\mu)^2/2\sigma^2}$ (\S3.6, p.~25). Chatterjee
identifies the correction term in the mixture identity (9) with the
Onsager correction (pp.~4--5).
\item \emph{Application} \sloc{Chatterjee2007-SpinGlassesSteinsMethod}{Theorem~1.1, p.~5; \S3.4, pp.~20--23}:
the law of $\ell_1$ is close to $M(\beta,h,r_1,1-q)$. With $f$ solving (15)
for these parameters ((38), p.~20), Gaussian integration by parts in
$(g_{1j})_j$ (Lemma~1.4, p.~9) and the overlap concentration (2) (p.~2),
followed by the standard conditional-mean identity
$\langle\sigma_1f\rangle=\langle\tanh(\beta\ell_1+h)f\rangle$ (p.~22), show
that
$\langle(\ell_1-r_1-\beta(1-q)\tanh(\beta\ell_1+h))f\rangle-(1-q)\langle\partial_xf\rangle
=-(1-q)\langle Tf\rangle$ is small in $L^2$ over the disorder. With
$u=\tanh(\beta x+h)$ and (14), this is Corollary~1.2 (p.~5):
$\langle\sigma_i\rangle\approx\tanh(\beta r_i+h)$.
\end{itemize}

 -\sigma^2\,Tf=(x-\mu-\sigma^2\tanh x)f-\sigma^2\partial_xf

for every $f$, and the Stein equation (15) for $u=\tanh$ reads

\paragraph{The earlier technique.}
AMS sample from the SK measure by discretizing the stochastic-localization
SDE of a Gibbs sample, with the drift computed by AMP; the analysis runs under
a planted model. In their (bold) notation, $\boldsymbol A\sim\mathsf{GOE}(n)$
has entries $N(0,1/n)$ off the diagonal and $N(0,2/n)$ on it,
$\mu_{\boldsymbol A}(\boldsymbol x)\propto e^{\frac\beta2\ip{\boldsymbol x}{\boldsymbol A\boldsymbol x}}$
on $\{-1,1\}^n$ \sloc{ElAlaouiMontanariSellke2022-SamplingSKAlgorithmicSL}{(1.1), p.~2},
and $\boldsymbol m(\boldsymbol A,\boldsymbol y)$ is the mean of its tilt by
$e^{\ip{\boldsymbol y}{\boldsymbol x}}$ ((1.2), p.~3).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Planted model} (\S4.1, p.~10). With $\bar\nu$ uniform on
 $\{-1,1\}^n$,
 \[
 \mu_{\rm pl}(d\boldsymbol x,d\boldsymbol A)\propto
 \exp\Bigl\{-\tfrac n4\bigl\lVert\boldsymbol A-\beta\boldsymbol x\boldsymbol x^{\mathsf T}/n\bigr\rVert_F^2\Bigr\}\,
 \bar\nu(d\boldsymbol x)\,d\boldsymbol A\qquad(4.1).
 \]
 Its $\boldsymbol x$-marginal is

 and $\mu_{\rm pl}(d\boldsymbol A)=\mu_{\mathsf{GOE}}(d\boldsymbol A)\,Z_{\mathsf{SK}}(\boldsymbol A)$ with
 $Z_{\mathsf{SK}}(\boldsymbol A)=2^{-n}\sum_{\boldsymbol x}\exp\{\frac\beta2\ip{\boldsymbol x}{\boldsymbol A\boldsymbol x}-\frac{\beta^2n}4\}$
 ((4.5)--(4.6)). Footnote~3 (p.~10) compares with the zero-diagonal model.
\item \emph{Path laws and likelihood} (pp.~11--12). $\mathbb Q$:
 $\boldsymbol A\sim\mu_{\mathsf{GOE}}$ and

 proven under $\P$ transfer to $\mathbb Q$'' (p.~12).

\item \emph{Fixed point} (Lemma~4.5, pp.~13--14).
 (c)~$\gamma_*=\beta^2(1-\operatorname{mmse}(\gamma_*+t))$ (4.21) has a
 nonnegative solution for $t\ge0$, unique for $t>0$ (p.~14),

\finding[A route to a Poincar\'e inequality via the inverse TAP Hessian]{A route to a Poincar\'e inequality from covariance bounds at stochastic-localization fields, via the inverse TAP Hessian}{eag-roadmap}

\paragraph{Summary.}
El Alaoui and Gaitonde (AG below)
\sloc{ElAlaouiGaitonde2022-CovarianceMatrixSK}{\S2, pp.~3--4} set out,
without proofs, a route, which they call possibly relevant to Poincar\'e and
log-Sobolev inequalities for the SK model at every $\beta<1$: at fields $y$
produced by stochastic localization, $\Cov(\mu_y)$ should be the inverse of
the negative TAP Hessian; its expected operator norm would be bounded given a
Frobenius approximation bound and uniform positive definiteness of that matrix
outside probability $O(1/n)$; and an operator-norm covariance bound along stochastic localization gives a
Poincar\'e inequality by the technique of Eldan--Shamir and Chen--Eldan.
The preprint carries out this route: it proves positivity of exactly this
matrix, in symmetrized form, at every approximate TAP root on the observation
path \ploc{(Proposition~5.1, p.~27)}{stability.tex:35--60}; at those fields,
with the TAP linear-response vector as test vector
\ploc{(\S6.4, pp.~45--48)}{residuals.tex:281--548}, it proves a covariance
bound \ploc{(Proposition~7.2, p.~56)}{consequences.tex:89--98} equivalent up
to constants to $\norm{\Cov_{\mu_h}(x)}\le C$; and it transfers that bound to
$\mu_0$ \ploc{(\S2, pp.~7--10)}{observation.tex:1--212}.
The preprint cites AG only for their zero-field Theorem~1.1
\ploc{(\S1.1, p.~4)}{introduction.tex:89--92}; it says neither that AG
proposed the route nor that Proposition~7.2 is an operator-norm covariance
bound at the posterior fields.
Grade: \grade{Partial}, because what AG contribute is a route, not proofs,
and their one concrete step, the Frobenius bound (2.7) with that positivity
at the mean of $\mu_y$, is not reused: the preprint
never forms $\mathcal H\Cov{}-I$, and its positivity holds at every approximate
root outside probability $e^{-an}$. The transfer step is Eldan--Shamir's and
Chen--Eldan's (the preprint credits Chen--Eldan), and the linear-response
formula Plefka's and Adhikari et al.'s, as AG say.

\paragraph{The earlier technique.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Linear response} ((2.2)--(2.5), p.~3). For a typical field,

\item \emph{Positivity} (p.~4). For $\beta$ small enough (e.g.\ below the AT

\paragraph{Summary.}
Huang, Mohanty, Rajaraman and Wu (HMRW) introduce stopped localization as
``a generic tool'' for weak Poincar\'e inequalities: stop a stochastic
localization at the first failure of a high-probability path estimate that
does not involve the test function, call a path bad if it
stops early or if its terminal measure fails the terminal inequality, and
split the retained variance into a good part, bounded by the terminal
inequality, and a bad part
\sloc{HuangEtAl2024-WeakPoincareSimulatedAnnealing}{\S2, p.~15; \S6.2, Lemmas~6.6 and~6.8, pp.~26--27; Lemmas~4.11 and~A.6, pp.~20 and~74}.
The preprint's transfer from posterior estimates to Theorem~1.1 has this
skeleton: a test-function-independent stopping time $\tau=T\wedge\tau_0$ at
the first possible loss of field stability
\ploc{(Corollary~5.2, p.~27; \S2.2, p.~7)}{stability.tex:62--97; observation.tex:45--51},
the bad event with its bound \ploc{(2.3)}{observation.tex:53--68}, stopped variance retention
\ploc{(2.4)}{observation.tex:70--88}, and the good/bad split
\ploc{(\S2.4, p.~10)}{observation.tex:188--205}.
The preprint does not cite HMRW. For the framework of its \S2 it cites
El~Alaoui--Montanari for the Gaussian channel and Chen--Eldan for ``the
variance/Dirichlet-form framework'' \ploc{(\S2, p.~7)}{observation.tex:3--9},
and Lehec only for the drift-energy identity
\ploc{(\S2.3, p.~8)}{observation.tex:104--105}. Chen--Eldan's annealing
theorem gives the good half, with the Dirichlet-form monotonicity (2.2) in
the role of their Proposition~48, but assumes the terminal gap almost surely,
with no exceptional paths
\sloc{ChenEldan2022-LocalizationSchemes}{Theorem~46 and Proposition~48, pp.~32--33};
the uncredited content is the stopping-and-exceptional-path scaffold.
Grade: \grade{Partial}, because that scaffold is reused while HMRW's defining
step, charging the bad part by $\operatorname{osc}(f)^2$ times its
probability, is replaced by an $f^2$-tilted entropy bound.

\paragraph{Setup.}
Section~\ref{sec:overview} of this document defines $\mu_h$, $\D_h$ and the
observation process $Y(t)$. This finding also needs:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Test function.} $\E_{\mu_0}f=0$, $\E_{\mu_0}f^2=1$ and
$d_0=\D_0(f)$. With $\E_t$ the expectation under $\mu_{Y(t)}$,
$M_t=\E_tf$, $N_t=\E_tf^2$ and $V_t=N_t-M_t^2=\Var_{\mu_{Y(t)}}(f)$
\ploc{(\S2.2, p.~8)}{observation.tex:70--74}.
\item \emph{Stopping.} $\tau_0$ is the first $t\le T$ with
$d_J(Y(t))\le\rho_0$, where $d_J(h)$ is the infimum of
$\norm{F_{J,h}(y)}/\sqrt n$ over pairs $(y,A)$ with $A$ near $V_y$ and
$\mathsf H_J(y,A)\not\succ cI$. Then
$\P(\tau_0\le T\mid J)\le e^{-an}$ on the matrix event of
\ploc{Proposition~5.1}{stability.tex:35--60}
\ploc{(Corollary~5.2 and its proof, p.~27)}{stability.tex:62--97}. Set
$\tau=T\wedge\tau_0$.
\item \emph{Bad paths.} With the terminal set
$\mathcal T_n(J)=\{h:\Var_{\mu_h}(g)\le2\D_h(g)\text{ for every }g\}$, which
$Y(T)$ misses with probability at most $e^{-cn}$
\ploc{(Proposition~8.3, (8.14), p.~63)}{terminal.tex:334--350}, a path is bad
if $\tau_0\le T$, or if $\tau_0>T$ and $Y(T)\notin\mathcal T_n(J)$. Then
$\P(\mathrm{bad})\le2e^{-an}$ \ploc{(2.3)}{observation.tex:60--67}; on good
paths $\tau=T$.
\item \emph{Tilted observation law.} $\P^f$ is the law of the observation path
when $X\sim f^2\mu_0$; its density on $\mathcal F_\tau$ is $N_\tau$
\ploc{(\S2.3, p.~8)}{observation.tex:90--97}.

\paragraph{The earlier technique.}
HMRW \cite{HuangEtAl2024-WeakPoincareSimulatedAnnealing} (arXiv v2,
November 2024) use stochastic localization with driving matrix $C_t$,
$\mu_t(x)\propto\mu_0(x)\exp(-\frac12\ip{x}{\Sigma_tx}+\ip{y_t}{x})$,
$\Sigma_t=\int_0^tC_s^2\,ds$ (Definition~6.2, p.~25). A
$(\rho_{\rm PI},\varepsilon)$-weak Poincar\'e inequality for Glauber dynamics
is $\Var_\pi[f]\le\rho_{\rm PI}^{-1}\mathcal E(f,f)+\varepsilon\operatorname{osc}(f)^2$
(Definition~A.1, p.~73).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Stopping} (\S6.2, p.~26). Approximate conservation of variance can
be ensured by stopping ``the localization scheme whenever it fails to hold''.
Lemma~6.6: for every $\varphi$,
\[
 \tau=T\wedge\inf\bigl\{t:\norm{C_t^{1/2}\Cov(\mu_t)C_t^{1/2}}_{\rm op}\ge K\bigr\}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \E\Var_{\mu_\tau}[\varphi]\ge e^{-TK}\Var_\mu[\varphi].
\]
Remark~6.7 notes that for variance retention alone the localization may
depend on $\varphi$, and extends the stopping to any rate-of-decay condition,
``by stopping the localization process whenever these conditions fail to
hold''. Lemma~6.8 uses the $\varphi$-independent $\tau$ of Lemma~6.6.
\item \emph{Exceptional paths} (Lemma~6.8, p.~27). If with probability
$1-\eta_1$ the covariance bound holds for all $t\in[0,T]$, and with
probability $1-\eta_2$ the measure $\mu_T$ satisfies a
$(\rho_{\rm PI},\delta)$-weak Poincar\'e inequality, then $\mu$ satisfies a
$(\rho_{\rm PI}e^{-TK},e^{TK}(\delta+\eta_1+\eta_2))$-weak Poincar\'e
inequality. Proof: use the decomposition $\mu=\E\mu_\tau$; $\tau=T$ with
probability $1-\eta_1$, so $\mu_\tau$ satisfies the terminal inequality with
probability at least $1-\eta_1-\eta_2$; conclude by Lemma~4.11.
\item \emph{Good/bad split} (Lemma~4.11, p.~20; Glauber version Lemma~A.6,
p.~74, which uses Fact~A.3, p.~73:
$\mathcal E_\pi(f,f)\ge\E_z\mathcal E_{\pi_z}(f,f)$). If $\E_z\Var_{\pi_z}[f]\ge C_{\rm Var}\Var_\pi[f]$ for all $f$ and
$\pi_z$ satisfies a $(\rho_{\rm PI},\delta)$-weak Poincar\'e inequality with
probability $1-\eta$, then $\pi$ satisfies a
$(\rho_{\rm PI}C_{\rm Var},(\delta+\eta)/C_{\rm Var})$-weak one. The proof
calls $z$ good if $\pi_z$ satisfies the inequality, applies it inside
$\E[\mathcal E_{\pi_z}(f,f)\one_{\rm good}]$, and bounds $\E[\Var_{\pi_z}[f]\one_{\text{not good}}]$ by
$\eta(\operatorname{osc}(f)^2+\sup\norm{\nabla f}^2)$, which is
$\eta\operatorname{osc}(f)^2$ in the Glauber form (Lemma~A.6).
\item \emph{Application} (\S7, p.~29). For spherical spin glasses (driving
matrix $\mathrm{Id}$, large constant $T$), a high-probability covariance
bound along the path (Lemma~7.6) and an endpoint weak
Poincar\'e inequality (Lemma~7.7) are plugged into Lemma~6.8.
\end{itemize}

Glauber dynamics for SK
\sloc{DaviesEtAl2026-WeakPoincareApproxSL}{Lemma~2.10, p.~9; Theorem~5.5, p.~22}
(see finding~\ref{f:dlss-weak-poincare}).

\noindent With $\pi_z=\mu_\tau$, $\eta=\eta_1+\eta_2$ and
$C_{\rm Var}=e^{-TK}$, HMRW's chain in the Glauber form (first line) and the
preprint's (second line) read
\begin{gather*}
 \mathcal E_\mu(f,f)\ge\rho_{\rm PI}\E\bigl[\Var_{\mu_\tau}[f]\one_{\rm good}\bigr]
 -\delta\rho_{\rm PI}\operatorname{osc}(f)^2,\qquad
 \E\bigl[\Var_{\mu_\tau}[f]\one_{\rm bad}\bigr]\le(\eta_1+\eta_2)\operatorname{osc}(f)^2;\\
 2d_0\ge2\E\D_{Y(T)}(f)\ge\E[\one_{\rm good}V_\tau],\qquad
 \E[\one_{\rm bad}V_\tau]\le\E[\one_{\rm bad}N_\tau]=\P^f(\mathrm{bad}),
\end{gather*}
each closed by its retention bound (Lemma~6.6, resp.\ (2.4)), where
$V_\tau=\Var_{\mu_{Y(\tau)}}(f)$ plays the role of $\Var_{\mu_\tau}[f]$. The
good halves coincide (a true Poincar\'e inequality is the case $\delta=0$);
the bad halves differ.

\paragraph{What is new in the preprint.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The bad part is bounded under the $f^2$-tilted observation law, not by
$\operatorname{osc}(f)^2$: $\P^f(\mathrm{bad})\le v/4$ uniformly over
normalized $f$ with $d_0\le1$, from the stopped drift-energy identity (2.5),
the square-reweighted mean estimate (1.6) (Proposition~7.3) applied before
$\tau$, the weighted Dirichlet identity (2.6), the entropy bound (2.7) and the
log-sum inequality \ploc{(\S2.3, pp.~8--9)}{observation.tex:90--160}.
This is what yields a Poincar\'e inequality rather than a weak one.
\item Variance retention through $u'\ge-C_{\mathrm d}(u+d_0)$, driven by
$\norm{\Cov_{\mu_h}(G,x)}^2\le C(\Var_{\mu_h}(G)+\D_h(G))$ and (2.2), in place
of a covariance cap with retention $e^{-TK}$.
\item The stopping criterion is field stability, the least normalized residual
$d_J(Y(t))$ of an unstable witness $(y,A)$, whose rarity is proved by a Kac--Rice-type
Gaussian integral (Proposition~5.1; finding~\ref{f:fmm-cfm-kac-rice}), not a
covariance norm.
\item Heat-bath Glauber dynamics on $\{-1,1\}^n$ for SK at every $\beta<1$,
with a true terminal Poincar\'e inequality at a large fixed time
(Proposition~8.3).
\end{itemize}

\finding[Weak Poincar\'e inequality for SK Glauber at \texorpdfstring{$\beta<1/2$}{beta<1/2}]{A weak Poincar\'e inequality for SK heat-bath Glauber dynamics at \texorpdfstring{$\beta<1/2$}{beta<1/2} via approximate stochastic localization}{dlss-weak-poincare}

\paragraph{Summary.}
Davies, Lee, Sandhu and Shi (DLSS; arXiv v1, July~9, 2026) proved by
approximate stochastic localization that for every $\beta<1/2$ and
$\delta>0$, with probability at least $1-\delta$ for large $n$, heat-bath Glauber dynamics for the zero-field
SK model satisfies the weak Poincar\'e inequality
$\Var_{\mu_0}(f)\le e^{C/\epsilon}\D_0(f)+\epsilon\operatorname{osc}(f)^2$ for
all $\epsilon\in(0,1)$ \sloc{DaviesEtAl2026-WeakPoincareApproxSL}{Cor.~1.5, p.~2},
the first functional inequality for this chain past the threshold
$\beta\approx0.295$ of Anari, Koehler and Vuong (DLSS, p.~1).
It is a weaker version, on half the range, of the preprint's
\ploc{Theorem~1.1 (p.~3)}{introduction.tex:33--42}, which implies it for
$C\ge\max(\log C_\beta,0)$.
DLSS was posted before both works the preprint cites for $\beta<1/2$, Wang
(cited version August~28, 2026) and Boban, Li and Oveis Gharan (BLO;
September~11, 2026), whose results also imply it on its range: BLO's constant
unscaled gap
\sloc{BobanLiOveisGharan2026-RankOnePerturbedTrickledownSK}{Thm.~1.5, Cor.~1.6, p.~5}
and the Poincar\'e inequality behind Wang's cited Theorem~1.1
\sloc{Wang2026-OptimalMixingGlauberSKBetaHalf}{Thm.~3.1, p.~8}.
The preprint does not cite DLSS: its history passes from Anari, Koehler and
Vuong directly to Wang and BLO \ploc{(\S1.1, p.~4)}{introduction.tex:75--87},
both of which discuss DLSS
\sloc{Wang2026-OptimalMixingGlauberSKBetaHalf}{p.~5},
\sloc{BobanLiOveisGharan2026-RankOnePerturbedTrickledownSK}{p.~7}.
Grade: \grade{Partial} (B): a weaker, partial-range version of the main
theorem, with none of its proof reused.
